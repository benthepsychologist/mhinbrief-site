## What this is

Therapy Bulletin is a maintained registry of the compliance and
professional-ethics obligations that govern licensed behavioural-health
practice — psychologists, psychotherapists, counsellors, social workers,
and marriage and family therapists. Canada first, the United States to
follow.

Regulators publish authoritative information organized around *their*
mandate. Blogs and vendor sites organize around your question, but are
frequently wrong and rarely dated. This site occupies the middle: organized
around the question, held to the source.

## What this is not

**This is not a law firm, and nothing here is legal advice.** Pages state
rules, quote the source verbatim, and list what to confirm and with whom.
They never apply the law to your facts — that judgment belongs to you and,
where it matters, to your college, your accountant, your insurer, or a
lawyer you retain.

## The discipline

Every substantive page follows the same three moves:

1. **State the rule** — plainly, in one or two sentences.
2. **Quote the source** — verbatim, with a link to the regulator, statute,
   or gazette that made it.
3. **Give verification steps** — the checks *you* perform, not conclusions
   reached for you.

Two stamps carry the trust:

- **Last verified** — the date we last checked the claim against its
  primary source. A verified non-change is information, so the stamp moves
  even when the rule doesn't.
- **Enacted vs. in force** — a rule that has been passed is not always a
  rule you must follow yet. The distinction is first-class here, because
  effective dates, not enactment dates, are the compliance trigger.

Claims we cannot yet trace to a primary source ship with an explicit
low-confidence flag or not at all. Where a regulator has published nothing,
"no published standard in this jurisdiction" is stated as a finding — not
papered over.

## The changelog

Every verified change to every entry — field changes, new records,
retirements — lands in the [changelog](/changelog/), dated and sourced,
append-only. The weekly rollup of that changelog is the Bulletin. Nothing
on this site changes silently.

Introduced bills are deliberately **not** shown on reference pages. A bill
that may die in committee is noise where you check what the law requires;
movement is reported in the Bulletin, and a rule reaches the reference
only on passage or coming into force.

## Corrections

If an entry is wrong, stale, or missing its receipt, that is a defect.
Corrections are logged in the changelog like any other change — visibly,
with the old text preserved.

## Who maintains this

Therapy Bulletin is maintained by a licensed, Canadian-registered clinical
psychologist. It is independent: no regulator, association, or vendor
directs its content.
