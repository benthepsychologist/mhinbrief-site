Retention rules look identical across regulators until you read them
closely. The period is the easy part; what differs — and what decides
whether a file can be destroyed — is **when the clock starts**, and each
college words that trigger differently. A rule that runs from the last
entry in a file behaves differently from one that runs from the last time
you saw the client.

The matrix below gives each regulator's period, its minor-client rule, and
its own words for when the clock begins, with the verbatim standard and
the date we last checked it. Where the governing instrument is a college
standard rather than a statute, the row says so — that distinction changes
who enforces it and how.
