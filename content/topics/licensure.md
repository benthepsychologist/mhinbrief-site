The same title does not mean the same thing in two provinces, and this is
the topic where that bites hardest. A clinician who moves — or who sees a
client across a provincial line — can find that the credential they hold
maps onto a different class, a different title, or no eligibility at all.

Three things vary independently, and reading only one of them will
mislead you. **The degree** that opens the door is not the same
everywhere. **The supervised-hour requirement** attached to it varies far
more than the degree does, and sometimes moves in the opposite direction —
a lower degree requirement can carry a substantially heavier experience
requirement, not a lighter one. And **the class structure itself** differs:
some regulators issue one general certificate, others build a matrix of
registrant type against practice authorization, and at least one province
treats the central activity as a reserved act attached to a permit rather
than as a profession with a title.

That last difference is the one most likely to catch someone out. Where a
jurisdiction regulates an *act* rather than a *title*, holding the right
credential in your own province tells you very little about whether you may
do the work in theirs.
